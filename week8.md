## Week 8 – Learning Experiment

### Learning Activities & Resources

This week, I conducted a learning experiment based on the Joomla and WordPress practical work I completed in previous weeks.

In previous practicals, I learned how to create and customise websites using two different content management systems. I created a Joomla website for a premium spirits store and a WordPress website based on an Authentic Matcha Guide. Through these practicals, I noticed that I usually understood a feature better when I actually tried to build it myself rather than only reading instructions or watching tutorials.

### Hypothesis

My hypothesis was:

**I will understand and remember CMS development better when I learn by building and experimenting with the website myself, compared with only reading or watching a tutorial.**

### Experiment

To test this hypothesis, I used two different learning methods.

**Method A – Tutorial-based learning**

First, I spent approximately 20 minutes reading instructions or watching a tutorial about a CMS feature. I tried to understand the steps without immediately implementing them.

After finishing, I wrote down the important steps that I could remember without looking back at the tutorial.

**Method B – Practical learning**

For another CMS feature, I spent approximately 20 minutes actually implementing the feature on one of my practice websites. I experimented with the settings, made changes, checked the results, and corrected problems when something did not work.

After finishing, I again wrote down the steps that I could remember without looking at any instructions.

### What I Measured

I compared the two methods based on:

* how many steps I could remember
* how confident I felt repeating the task
* how often I needed to check instructions again
* whether I understood why each step was necessary

### Results

After completing the experiment, I found that practical learning was more effective for me.

When I only read or watched instructions, I could understand what was being demonstrated, but I sometimes forgot the exact steps afterwards.

When I performed the task myself, I remembered more of the process. Making mistakes and solving problems also helped me understand why particular settings or files were needed.

For example, during my previous Joomla and WordPress practical work, I encountered problems with layouts, images, themes and styling. Solving these problems myself helped me understand the CMS more deeply than simply following instructions.

Therefore, the results of my experiment supported my hypothesis that practical, hands-on learning helps me understand and remember web development concepts better.

### Learning Insights

The most important thing I learned from this experiment is that I learn technical skills more effectively by actively using them.

Tutorials are still useful when I encounter something new, but I should not depend only on watching or reading them. After learning a new concept, I should immediately try to implement it myself.

I also learned that making mistakes can be useful. When something did not work correctly in Joomla or WordPress, I had to investigate the problem and test different solutions. This made the solution easier to remember.

In future practicals, I will use tutorials mainly as a starting point and then spend more of my learning time experimenting and building features myself.
